LAND USE AND HUMAN IMPACT KNOWLEDGE

Land-use change can alter habitat availability, habitat connectivity and ecological processes.

Agricultural intensification can simplify landscapes and reduce habitat diversity when natural or semi-natural vegetation is removed.

Monoculture systems contain a limited number of crop species and can provide fewer habitat types than diversified agricultural landscapes.

Agroforestry combines trees with crops or livestock and can increase structural diversity and provide additional habitat. Its suitability depends on local climate, soil, crop requirements and available water.

Intercropping involves growing multiple crop species together and can increase crop diversity compared with monoculture systems.

Habitat restoration should consider native species, local climate, water availability and existing land use.

Important relationships:

Monoculture + low habitat diversity
→ reduced habitat complexity.

Diversified vegetation + suitable local conditions
→ potentially greater habitat resources and ecological connectivity.

Land-use recommendations should consider soil, rainfall, temperature and biodiversity together.
