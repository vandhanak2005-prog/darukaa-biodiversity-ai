SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE 1

Recommendation: Introduce cover crops or legume intercropping

Source organization: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO)

Source: Soil Organic Cover - Conservation Agriculture

Evidence:
FAO explains that cover crops can improve soil properties, protect soil during fallow periods, recycle nutrients, improve soil structure and increase biodiversity in agro-ecosystems.

Relevant metrics:
Soil organic carbon, soil moisture, soil structure, biodiversity

Source URL:
https://www.fao.org/conservation-agriculture/in-practice/soil-organic-cover/en/


SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE 2

Recommendation: Improve water conservation

Source organization: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO)

Source: The Importance of Soil Organic Matter

Evidence:
FAO explains that soil organic matter affects soil structure, moisture-holding capacity, infiltration and biological activity. Crop residues can improve infiltration, reduce evaporation and increase soil moisture.

Relevant metrics:
Soil moisture, water availability, organic matter

Source URL:
https://www.fao.org/4/a0100e/a0100e08.htm


SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE 3

Recommendation: Increase crop and habitat diversity

Source organization: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO)

Source: The 10 Elements of Agroecology - Diversity

Evidence:
FAO describes diversification through intercropping, crop rotations, agroforestry and other approaches as ways to increase biological diversity and support ecosystem functions such as pollination and nutrient cycling.

Relevant metrics:
Species richness, habitat diversity, pollinator resources

Source URL:
https://www.fao.org/agroecology/overview/the-10-elements-of-agroecology/diversity/en/


SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE 4

Recommendation: Increase crop and habitat diversity

Source organization: Scientific Advances / peer-reviewed research

Source: Agricultural diversification promotes multiple ecosystem services without compromising yield

Evidence:
A review of agricultural diversification research found that diversification can enhance biodiversity and ecosystem services including pollination, pest control, nutrient cycling, soil fertility and water regulation. Effects can vary according to context.

Relevant metrics:
Biodiversity, pollination, soil fertility, water regulation

Source URL:
https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba1715


SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE 5

Recommendation: Cover crops

Source organization: Agriculture, Ecosystems & Environment

Source: Soil organic carbon fractions in response to soil, environmental and agronomic factors under cover cropping systems: A global meta-analysis

Evidence:
A global meta-analysis reported increases in several soil organic carbon fractions under cover cropping, with the magnitude varying according to soil, environmental and management conditions.

Relevant metrics:
Soil organic carbon, microbial biomass carbon

Source URL:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2023.108591


SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE 6

Recommendation: Create habitat corridors and native vegetation patches

Source organization: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO)

Source: Crop Diversification for Pollinator Conservation

Evidence:
FAO summarizes research indicating that intercropping, flowering crops and other diversification measures can increase floral resources and landscape connectivity that benefit pollinators. Effects can vary among species.

Relevant metrics:
Pollinator biodiversity, habitat connectivity, habitat diversity

Source URL:
https://www.fao.org/agroecology/in-action/detail/crop-diversification-for-pollinator-conservation/en
