BIODIVERSITY KNOWLEDGE

Biodiversity includes the variety of organisms, species and habitats within an ecosystem.

Species richness refers to the number of different species present in an area.

Habitat diversity refers to the variety of habitats available to organisms. Greater habitat diversity can provide different resources and ecological niches for plants, insects, birds and other organisms.

Agricultural monocultures can reduce habitat complexity compared with more diverse agricultural systems.

Increasing vegetation diversity and providing flowering or native vegetation can create additional resources for pollinators and other organisms.

Biodiversity should not be assessed using species richness alone. Habitat diversity, land use, vegetation structure, water availability and human pressures should also be considered.

Important relationships:

Low habitat diversity + monoculture
→ fewer habitat types and ecological niches.

Greater vegetation diversity + suitable habitat
→ potentially greater habitat resources for biodiversity.
